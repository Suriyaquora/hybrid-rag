# VEL PARI — The Epic (Pan-Indian Multi-Part Film Plan)

*Working title:* **வேள்பாரி — Veera Yuga Nayagan**
*Source:* Sangam poetry (Purananuru, Kapilar) and the novel *Veera Yuga Nayagan Vel Pari* by **Su. Venkatesan**.
> Rights: a film adaptation of the novel requires a licence from the author and publisher. Everything below is a development plan, not a licensed script. Scenes flagged *(tradition)* are legend, not recorded history.

## 1. Logline
A generous hill chieftain who gave his chariot to a jasmine creeper refuses to bow to the combined might of the Chera, Chola and Pandya crowns — and a poet, Kapilar, makes sure the world never forgets what he stood for.

## 2. Format — a three-part theatrical saga (+ optional series)
| Part | Title | Runtime | Spine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Parambu** — *The Gift* | ~170 min | Land, people, the Kapilar–Pari friendship, jasmine & chariot, the Moovendar's envy, the ultimatum |
| **II** | **Moovendar** — *The Siege* | ~180 min | Council of the hills, guerrilla war in the forest, monsoon, parrots & granaries *(tradition)*, cracks in the alliance |
| **III** | **Mullai** — *The Last Jasmine* | ~160 min | Deception *(tradition)*, the fall, daughters' lament (Purananuru 112), Kapilar's vow, legacy |
Release 6–8 months apart. Optional **8-episode streaming cut** (≈55 min each) = the same material with extra tribal-culture side arcs.

The one-hour documentary in this folder (`index.html`) maps onto the saga as a **proof-of-tone / pitch reel**: Prologue+Ch.I-II → Part I, Ch.III-VI → Part I/II, Ch.VII-VIII → Part II, Ch.IX-Epilogue → Part III.

## 3. Pan-Indian strategy
- **Shot once in Tamil** (authenticity of verse, idiom, music); **dubbed + lip-adjusted** Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Hindi; subtitled in Bengali, Marathi, English.
- Cast leads for regional pull: Chera (Malayalam), Chola (Tamil), Pandya (Tamil/Telugu), a Kannada & Hindi-market anchor in a supporting war-chief role — chosen on *fit*, not just marquee.
- Universal themes for every market: **generosity vs. empire, friendship of power and art, ecological sovereignty of tribal land.**
- Music: Tamil folk + Carnatic pentatonic (Mohanam-family) core, western orchestral for scale. Sangam verses sung on-screen, never just subtitled.

## 4. Principal characters
| Character | Arc |
|---|---|
| **Vel Pari** | Generosity as strength → refuses vassalage → destroyed by his own hospitality |
| **Kapilar** | Wandering poet → Pari's brother-in-spirit → keeper of his memory; fulfils the vow to the daughters |
| **Angavai & Sangavai** | Daughters; carry the lament of the lost hill |
| **The Three Kings** (Chera, Chola, Pandya) | Rivals united by envy; alliance fractures in the siege |
| **The hill council** | Elders, trackers, healers — the people as protagonist |
| **Malaiyaman** | The honourable ruler who takes the daughters in marriage |

## 5. Sequence outline (maps to documentary chapters)
1. **Prologue** — Tamilakam, the three crowns, the chieftain with no crown.
2. **Parambu Nadu** — 300 villages, hearth-lamp ritual, ecology as defence.
3. **Poet & Chieftain** — Kapilar's arrival; the friendship that carries the epic.
4. **Jasmine & Chariot** — *Mullaikku Ther Koduthaan*: the signature image of the franchise.
5. **Three Crowns** — the pact; politics told as intimate court scenes.
6. **Ultimatum** — envoys, hospitality as courtesy and trap.
7. **Council of the Hills** — guerrilla strategy: terrain, false trails, seed-grain.
8. **Siege** — monsoon war, fire in the lower forests, alliance cracks (centrepiece of Part II).
9. **Parrots & Granaries** *(tradition)* — Kapilar's flocks; hunger as the true battlefield.
10. **The Fall** *(tradition)* — deception at the threshold of hospitality.
11. **Vow of Kapilar** — the daughters, Purananuru 112 lament, marriage to Malaiyaman, Kapilar's end.
12. **Epilogue** — the jasmine standing on its own in the shape of the vanished chariot.

## 6. Production design
- **Locations:** Western Ghats / Pachaimalai / Kolli Hills for Parambu; sets for three court cities; VFX extension of a stylised Parambu peak.
- **Look:** warm firelight villages vs. cold, geometric crown camps; each crown owns a colour (Chera crimson, Chola tiger-gold, Pandya sea-teal) — the same palette used in the documentary.
- **Action:** forest/guerrilla combat, practical stunts + VFX elephants; avoid gore-first spectacle — the moral stakes are the spectacle.
- **Authenticity board:** Tamil scholars, Sangam-literature advisors, tribal-community consultants, historians (flag legend vs. record in every sequence).

## 8. Risks
- **Rights & fidelity** to a beloved novel → author as creative consultant.
- **Historical sensitivity** (caste, tribe, dynasty pride) → advisory board, no villainising of any living community.
- **Length / pacing** across three parts → each part must stand alone and end on a poem.
- **Dubbing quality** → budget for native-speaker adaptation, not literal translation.
